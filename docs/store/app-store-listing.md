Paste into App Store Connect. No em-dashes in customer-facing text.

**App name** (30 characters max): `Curious` (App Store names must be unique. If it is taken, try `Curious: Learn More` or `Curious for Safari`. The name under the icon stays Curious.)

**Subtitle** (30 max): `Learn more without leaving` (26)

**Category:** Reference. Secondary: Education.

**Promotional text** (170 max):

```
Select a word in Safari and see a short Wikipedia definition and pictures right beside it. Your page stays exactly where it was.
```

**Description:**

```
Curious is a Safari extension for readers. Select a word or phrase on any web page and click Learn more. A small card opens beside the word with a short definition from Wikipedia. Your place on the page never moves.

• A two-sentence Wikipedia definition, with a link to the full article
• Pictures from the article, each with its author, license and source
• Follow-up lookups: select a word inside the card to look it up too, then go Back
• Web and YouTube buttons that open a small window you can close to return to your spot
• Light and dark mode that follow your Mac
• Keyboard friendly: Option+L opens the card for your selection, Esc closes it

GETTING STARTED
Open Curious once, then turn on the Curious extension in Safari Settings > Extensions. On any page, click the Curious button in the Safari toolbar to turn it on for that page. To pick the search engine for the Web button, open Curious settings from Safari Settings > Extensions.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
Curious only runs on a page after you click its toolbar button. Only the words you select are sent to Wikipedia, without cookies or the page address. No account, no analytics, no ads, no tracking. Open source under the MIT License.

Definitions and pictures come from Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons and keep their original licenses. Curious is not affiliated with the Wikimedia Foundation.
```

## App Privacy (privacy label)

Answer **"No, we do not collect data from this app."** Result: **Data Not Collected.**

Reason: Apple counts data as collected when it is sent off the device in a way that the developer or its partners can access beyond servicing the request in real time. Curious has no server and no partners. The only thing that leaves the device is the selected term, sent straight to Wikipedia's public API to answer that one lookup and never retained by the developer.

## App Review notes

```
Curious is a Safari Web Extension. The app window only explains how to enable it.

To test:
1. Launch Curious and click "Quit and Open Safari Settings". In Safari Settings > Extensions, enable Curious.
2. Open any article, for example https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioluminescence or a news site.
3. Click the Curious button in the Safari toolbar and allow it for the site when Safari asks.
4. Select a word, for example "plankton", and click the "Learn more" button that appears beside it. A card shows a Wikipedia definition.
5. Click Images to see pictures with credits. Select a word inside the card and click "Look up" to follow it; "Back" returns. Press Esc to close.
6. Web opens a small search window. In Safari, choose the search engine once in Curious settings (Safari Settings > Extensions > Curious > Settings).

No account or login is needed. The extension sends only the selected term to en.wikipedia.org and stores only the search engine choice locally. No data is collected.
```
