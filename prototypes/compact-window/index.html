A little light in the dark

Original sample article · 3 min read

At the edge of the ocean, a passing wave sometimes carries its own light. The glow is brief: a blue line traced across the water, gone before the next wave arrives.

This phenomenon is called bioluminescence. Living organisms produce light through chemical reactions. A disturbance in the water can turn a quiet patch of sea into a field of tiny signals.

Curiosity, without losing the thread

A single unfamiliar idea can open several paths. You might want a short definition, a photograph, or a video showing the light moving through the surf. You should be able to explore one of those paths and then return to the sentence that started it.

Try selecting marine snow or symbiosis. The card keeps your term ready while the real search website opens beside your reading.

Back at the shore

When you return, this article should be exactly where you left it. The experiment is successful if the browser window feels temporary, the source feels familiar, and resuming your reading takes very little effort.
